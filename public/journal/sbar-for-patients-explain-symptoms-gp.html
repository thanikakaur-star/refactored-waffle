SBAR for Patients: How to Explain Your Symptoms Clearly in a 10-Minute GP Appointment

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

If you have ever left a GP appointment realising you forgot the one thing that mattered, you are not alone. Clinicians use a simple structure to communicate quickly and clearly, and you can borrow it. It is called SBAR.

What is SBAR?

SBAR stands for Situation, Background, Assessment and Recommendation. It is widely used in healthcare to hand over information safely and quickly, for example when a nurse updates a doctor. Patients are rarely taught it, but the same shape works well when you are the one explaining. In this patient version we swap the last step for a Request, because you are saying what you are hoping for.

The four steps in plain English

S: Situation (why I am here today)

One or two sentences, stated first. For example: "I have had headaches most days for two months and I would like to understand why."

B: Background (what led up to it)

When it started, what changed around then, anything that makes it better or worse, and what you have already tried, including medicines and supplements.

A: Assessment (how it affects me)

How it affects your sleep, work, energy and daily life, any pattern you have noticed, and anything that worries you. Specific details such as "I have missed three days of work this month" are easier to act on than "it is really bad".

R: Request (what I am hoping for)

Be specific about what would help: an examination, a test, a treatment discussion, a referral, a plan with a follow-up date, or a question about using your Right to Choose.

A worked example

S: "I have had headaches most days for two months." B: "They started after a very stressful period at work. Paracetamol barely helps and they are worst in the afternoon." A: "I am missing work and cannot concentrate after lunch, and it is affecting my sleep." R: "I would like to understand what is causing them and whether I need any tests or a referral."

Make it even easier

Write your four answers down before you go, and bring them as a single page. Handing over a brief means nothing gets lost if the appointment is short. Our guide to preparing for a 10-minute GP appointment goes further, and Kaur Care can turn your SBAR notes into a clean one-page brief for free.

A note on safety

SBAR is about being heard in routine appointments. If symptoms are severe or sudden, such as chest pain, difficulty breathing, signs of a stroke or heavy bleeding, call 999. For urgent advice that is not an emergency, use NHS 111.
