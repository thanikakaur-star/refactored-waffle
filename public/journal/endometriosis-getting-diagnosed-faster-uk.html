Endometriosis: How to Get Taken Seriously and Diagnosed Faster

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Endometriosis affects around one in ten women and those assigned female at birth, yet getting a diagnosis can take years. Preparation and persistence genuinely help shorten that journey.

What it can feel like

Common experiences include painful periods that disrupt daily life, chronic pelvic pain, pain during or after sex, pain when going to the toilet during your period, and sometimes difficulty conceiving. Pain that is dismissed as "just bad periods" can be worth a closer look.

Being heard at the GP

Go in with specifics: where the pain is, how long you have had it, how it tracks with your cycle, a pain score, what you have tried, and the impact on work, study and relationships. Ask clearly whether endometriosis could be the cause and what the next step is.

If progress stalls

It is reasonable to return if symptoms continue, to ask about referral to a gynaecologist, and in England to ask about your Right to Choose, which can shorten waits for a specialist assessment. Keeping a symptom diary across a few cycles strengthens your case.

When to seek help sooner

Sudden severe pelvic pain, pain with fever or feeling faint need urgent help, contact your GP or NHS 111, and call 999 for severe symptoms.

Walk in prepared

Build a clear 1-page GP brief or a Right to Choose referral letter in a couple of minutes, then take it to your appointment. Free, and your details stay on your device.
