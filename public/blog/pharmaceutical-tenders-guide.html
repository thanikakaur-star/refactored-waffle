How Pharmaceutical Tenders Work in Public Healthcare

Pharmaceutical tenders are how public health systems buy medicines at scale — from NHS medicine frameworks to EU national tenders to WHO and UNICEF essential-medicines procurement. Because many tenders are for off-patent, multi-source drugs, they're often intensely price-competitive, while prequalification and quality assurance remain the gatekeeper to bidding at all. Here's how the category works.

Who buys pharmaceuticals through tenders

  • NHS — medicines procurement through national and regional frameworks
  • National health ministries across the EU — via TED Europa
  • WHO and UNICEF — essential medicines and vaccines for global health programmes, typically requiring WHO prequalification
  • Gavi and The Global Fund — finance and coordinate large medicine and vaccine purchases for lower-income countries
  • US federal agencies (VA, DoD) — via SAM.gov

Generics and multi-source competition

Many pharmaceutical tenders are for off-patent medicines where several manufacturers can supply an equivalent product. This makes these tenders intensely price-driven — the tender essentially becomes a competition on manufacturing cost and reliability rather than clinical differentiation. Suppliers who benchmark pricing against published award data avoid both overpricing (losing the tender) and underpricing (winning at a loss).

Quality assurance is the real gatekeeper

Before price even matters, a pharmaceutical supplier has to clear the quality bar:

  • WHO Prequalification for global health tenders
  • National regulatory approval (e.g. MHRA in the UK, EMA in the EU, FDA in the US)
  • GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) certification
  • Consistent, auditable supply chain documentation

Supply security is now a scored factor

Medicine shortages in recent years have pushed buyers to weight supply chain resilience more heavily — multiple manufacturing sites, diversified active ingredient sourcing, and demonstrated ability to meet volume commitments are increasingly part of the evaluation, not just price.

Finding pharmaceutical tenders

Pharma tenders are scattered across NHS, TED Europa, WHO/UNICEF and SAM.gov, each with different formats, languages and classification systems — the same fragmentation problem covered in manual tracking vs a platform.

Frequently asked questions

Are pharmaceutical tenders mostly about price?

For generics and multi-source medicines, price is often the dominant factor once quality requirements are met. For novel or single-source medicines, other factors carry more weight.

What's the fastest way to fail a pharma tender?

Missing a quality or regulatory requirement — these are usually pass/fail gates before price is even considered.

Do generics manufacturers need WHO prequalification?

For global health tenders (WHO, UNICEF, Global Fund, Gavi), yes, in most cases. For national tenders, national regulatory approval is the requirement instead.
