Baby Red Flags: When to Call 999, NHS 111 or Your GP (UK Parents' Guide)

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Newborns and babies can change quickly, and it is hard to know what is normal. This calm guide covers the main signs that mean getting help, and which service to use. The most important rule: you know your baby best, and you will never be wasting anyone's time by asking.

Call 999 if your baby

  • Is struggling to breathe, breathing very fast, or has long pauses in breathing.
  • Is very floppy, limp, hard to wake, or not responding to you.
  • Has a fit (seizure) that does not stop, or a first-time fit.
  • Has blue, grey or very pale skin or lips.
  • Has a rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it.

In any emergency, call 999 straight away. Do not wait to see whether things settle.

Get same-day help (NHS 111 or your GP) if

  • Your baby is under 3 months and has a temperature of 38°C or higher, or is 3 to 6 months with a temperature of 39°C or higher. Check the NHS guidance on fever in young children and act promptly.
  • Your baby is feeding much less than usual, or refusing feeds.
  • There are signs of dehydration, such as noticeably fewer wet nappies, a dry mouth, or a sunken soft spot on the top of the head.
  • Your baby is unusually sleepy, irritable, or has a high-pitched or weak cry.
  • There is persistent vomiting, especially if green, or blood in the stools.
  • Your baby has jaundice (yellowing of skin or eyes) in the first weeks of life.

Not sure which to use?

Use NHS 111 online or by phone. They can assess your baby's symptoms and direct you to the right place, and they will tell you if it needs an ambulance. Your health visitor can also help with feeding, sleep, development and general worries between appointments, and your baby's red book (personal child health record) is a useful thing to keep to hand.

Things that are common and usually settle

Many babies have blocked noses, sneezing, hiccups, milk spots, occasional spit-up, and irregular sleep patterns. These are common, and a pharmacist can often help with minor problems. See our guide on kids' earache, sore throat and rashes and what a pharmacist can help with.

Prepare for the call or appointment

Note down when symptoms started, your baby's temperature, how much they are feeding, wet and dirty nappies, and any rash. Photos can help. Having this ready means you can explain clearly even when you are tired and worried.

This is general information, not medical advice. If you are worried about your baby, seek help.
