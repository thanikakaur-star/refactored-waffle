When You Feel Dismissed: Getting Taken Seriously About Long-Term Symptoms

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Few things are more wearing than knowing something is wrong and feeling brushed off. If you have been told it is probably stress, or sent away without a plan more than once, this is not about being pushy, it is about giving the system what it needs to take you seriously.

Bring evidence, not just a description

Track your symptoms over a few weeks: when they happen, how severe (a 0 to 10 scale helps), what triggers or eases them, and crucially the impact on your work, sleep and daily life. Patterns on paper are harder to dismiss than a description in a rushed appointment.

Ask specific, answerable questions

Questions that move things forward include: "What else could this be?", "What would need to be true for you to investigate further?", "What are we ruling out, and how?", and "What is the plan if this does not improve by a set date?" These invite a next step rather than reassurance.

Ask for it to be documented

It is reasonable to ask that your symptoms and your concern are recorded in your notes, including that you asked about further investigation. A clear record helps continuity and makes the next appointment build on this one.

Use your right to a second opinion

You can ask for a second opinion, and in England you can use your Right to Choose to be referred to a clinically appropriate NHS provider. A fresh set of eyes is a normal part of medicine, not a criticism of anyone.

Be kind to yourself, and know the red lines

Persisting is tiring, and it is not your fault that it is hard. Keep going, bring someone with you if it helps, and do not downplay how it affects you. If symptoms are severe or rapidly worsening, chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness, heavy bleeding, do not wait to be believed, call NHS 111 or 999. And if things feel hopeless, the Samaritans (116 123) are there any time.

Walk in prepared

Build a clear 1-page GP brief or a Right to Choose referral letter in a couple of minutes, then take it to your appointment. Free, and your details stay on your device.
