How to Prepare for a 10-Minute GP Appointment (Especially for Endo, PCOS & Chronic Symptoms)

4 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Ten minutes is not long, especially when you are in pain and the thing you most wanted to say slips your mind. For conditions like endometriosis and PCOS, which are so often dismissed or delayed, preparation is how you get heard.

Before you go: get it on one page

Write your main concern in a sentence, when it started, and how it has changed. Pick the one or two things that matter most and lead with those. Bring dates, measurements and a short symptom diary.

For endometriosis, PCOS and cycle symptoms

Specifics help. Note your cycle length and how regular it is. For pain, where it is, a 0 to 10 score at its worst, and how it tracks with your cycle. For bleeding, how heavy (flooding, clots, products used per hour) and the effect on your energy. List what you have already tried, and what you want to discuss, including options like hormonal treatment, a scan or a gynaecology referral. Tiredness with heavy periods is worth flagging, as it can prompt an iron check.

During the appointment

Lead with your main concern in the first sentence. Ask your questions while you are there. Before you leave, confirm the plan: what happens next, what to watch for, and when to come back or seek urgent help.

After

Jot down what was agreed, any medicine names and the follow-up date while it is fresh. If a first appointment does not get you answers, it is reasonable to go back; persistent pain deserves follow-up.

Walk in prepared

Build a clear 1-page GP brief or a Right to Choose referral letter in a couple of minutes, then take it to your appointment. Free, and your details stay on your device.
