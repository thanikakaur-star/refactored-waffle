Stuck on a Long NHS ADHD or Autism Waiting List? How Right to Choose Works in England

4 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

If you have been told the wait for an ADHD or autism assessment is measured in years, you are not imagining it. What far fewer people are told is that in England you have a legal right to choose who assesses you, and that choice can shorten the wait, at no cost to you.

What Right to Choose is

Under the NHS Choice Framework in England, when your GP refers you for a routine assessment you can usually ask to be referred to any provider that holds an NHS contract for that service, not only your local one. Several independent providers offer NHS-funded ADHD and autism assessments with shorter waits. You still pay nothing; it is NHS care delivered somewhere with more capacity.

Right to Choose applies to routine referrals in England. It does not apply in urgent or crisis mental-health situations, and local rules can vary, so confirm current acceptance with your GP and the provider.

Step by step

1. Book a GP appointment and say you would like a referral and want to use your right to choose your provider. 2. Research a provider that explicitly offers NHS Right to Choose assessments for your age group, and note their details. 3. Ask the GP to refer you there. 4. If the practice is unsure, calmly mention the NHS Choice Framework and bring a short written request. 5. Put it in writing, a clear letter naming the framework, the condition and your chosen provider.

What to expect

Waiting times still vary by provider, so compare before you choose. Once referred, the provider will contact you about questionnaires and next steps. Keep a note of dates and who you spoke to. Right to Choose is a route, not a guarantee of a particular outcome, and acceptance can depend on your local Integrated Care Board, but for many people it turns a multi-year wait into a far shorter one.
