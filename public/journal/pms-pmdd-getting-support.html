PMS and PMDD: Getting Support, Not Just 'It's Hormonal'

4 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Feeling low, irritable or anxious in the days before your period is common, but when it seriously affects your mood, relationships or ability to function, it deserves proper support rather than a shrug.

PMS and PMDD

Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) covers the physical and emotional symptoms many people get before a period. Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a more severe form, where mood symptoms are intense and disruptive, and it is recognised and treatable.

Tracking the pattern

The key clue is timing: symptoms that reliably appear in the run-up to your period and ease once it starts. Tracking your mood and symptoms against your cycle for two or three months gives your GP a clear picture.

Getting help

Take your symptom diary to your GP and describe the impact honestly, including on work and relationships. There are approaches that can help, and you are entitled to have the conversation taken seriously.

An important note

If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out now: call NHS 111 and select the mental health option, contact the Samaritans on 116 123, or call 999 in an emergency. You deserve support straight away.
