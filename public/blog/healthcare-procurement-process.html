The Healthcare Procurement Process, Step by Step

The healthcare procurement process is the sequence of steps a health buyer follows to purchase goods or services — from first identifying a need to managing the delivered contract. Understanding it helps suppliers know when to act and where they can influence the outcome.

Step 1: Identify the need

A trust, ministry or agency identifies something it needs to buy and secures the budget. Suppliers rarely see this stage, but engaging early — through market engagement events or prior information notices — can shape the eventual specification.

Step 2: Plan and specify

The buyer decides how to buy: a one-off tender, a call-off from a framework, or a Dynamic Purchasing System. They write the specification and evaluation criteria — the rules you'll be scored against.

Step 3: Publish the tender

The opportunity is published on an e-procurement portal (TED, Find a Tender, SAM.gov, UNGM and others). This is the moment suppliers need to catch it — miss it and you may have only days to respond. See how to find NHS tenders.

Step 4: Bid

Suppliers submit their responses against the specification. Strong bids evidence quality, capacity and value — not just the lowest price. See bid-writing tips and how to price a bid.

Step 5: Evaluate and award

The buyer scores each bid against the published criteria and awards the contract, publishing an award notice naming the winner and (often) the value — valuable intelligence for your next bid.

Step 6: Manage the contract

Delivery, performance management and eventual re-tender. Knowing when a contract expires tells you when the next opportunity will appear — which is why tracking award data matters.

Where suppliers gain or lose

Most suppliers engage far too late — at Step 3, with little time left. The winners engage early and monitor continuously, which is the core problem an aggregated feed solves.

Frequently asked questions

What are the stages of the healthcare procurement process?

Broadly: identify the need, plan and specify, publish the tender, receive bids, evaluate and award, then manage the contract.

When should suppliers get involved?

As early as possible — ideally before the tender is published, through market engagement. At minimum, catch the tender the day it's published to leave time for a strong bid.

What is the difference between a framework and a one-off tender?

A one-off tender buys a specific requirement once; a framework pre-approves suppliers to be called off repeatedly over time.
