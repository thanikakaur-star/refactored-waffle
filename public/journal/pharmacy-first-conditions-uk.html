12 Things You Can Get Sorted at a UK Pharmacy Today (Without the 8 AM GP Queue)

6 min read · Kaur Care Journal

You wake up, something hurts, and the only plan is to join the 8 AM phone scramble for a GP appointment. Here is the thing many people still do not realise: for a growing list of everyday conditions, you can walk into a pharmacy and be seen the same day, for free, no appointment and no GP referral.

This is NHS Pharmacy First in England. Since early 2024 your community pharmacist has been able to assess, and where appropriate supply treatment including some prescription-only medicines, for a defined set of conditions. It is quick, it is private (most pharmacies have a consultation room), and it takes real pressure off the GP queue.

The seven your pharmacist can treat right now

These seven pathways are available nationwide. Age and eligibility rules apply, and your pharmacist will check them with you.

1. Urinary tract infections (UTIs)

For women aged 16 to 64 with uncomplicated symptoms. Not suitable in pregnancy, for men, or if you feel very unwell or have back or flank pain, in which case you will be pointed to a GP or 111.

2. Sore throat

For anyone aged 5 and over. The pharmacist checks whether it is likely to need treatment.

3. Earache in children (acute otitis media)

For children aged 1 to 17. This one must be done in person, so a distance-selling online pharmacy cannot provide it.

4. Sinusitis

For anyone aged 12 and over.

5. Impetigo

For anyone aged 1 and over with the tell-tale crusty skin infection.

6. Infected insect bites

For anyone aged 1 and over, when a bite has become infected.

7. Shingles

For adults aged 18 and over. Get seen quickly, as treatment works best started early.

All seven are free on the NHS and usually dealt with the same day. If you are not eligible, the pharmacist will tell you the right next step rather than leave you stuck.

Five more being rolled out

Through late 2026, NHS pharmacy services have been expanding. Depending on your area and your pharmacy, you may also be able to get help with:

  • Adult outer ear infection (otitis externa)
  • Children's hay fever and seasonal allergy symptoms
  • Mild to moderate acne
  • Episodic migraine in adults
  • Mild skin and soft-tissue infections, and scabies

These newer pathways are still being introduced and availability varies from place to place, so treat them as "worth asking about" rather than guaranteed. A quick call to your local pharmacy, or a check of current NHS England guidance, will confirm what is on offer near you.

When the pharmacy will send you onward

Pharmacists are trained to spot when something needs more than they can give. If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, a very high fever, a stiff neck, a spreading infection, or you simply feel very unwell, that is a job for 111 or 999, not a pharmacy counter. Trust that steer.

How to make the visit quick

Jot down when your symptoms started, what you have already tried, and any medicines or allergies. If it turns out you do need the GP instead, a clear one-page summary of the same details will make that appointment count too.
