On an NHS Waiting List? What to Do While You Wait (and Your 18-Week Rights)

6 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Being told the wait is months, or longer, is hard, especially when you are in pain or worried. You cannot make the list shorter on your own, but there is a lot you can do to stay safe, stay on track and, in some cases, move faster. This guide is for England.

Check that your referral is where it should be

  • Use the NHS App, or ask your GP surgery, to confirm your referral was sent and to which service.
  • Contact the hospital's booking team to check they have received it and ask for an estimate of the wait.
  • Look at the "My Planned Care" website for typical waiting times at different hospitals, which can help you decide whether to ask about other providers.

Know your 18-week right

Under the NHS Constitution, you have the right to start consultant-led treatment within 18 weeks of a referral for a non-urgent condition. When that cannot be met, the NHS should take reasonable steps to offer you alternatives. In practice, many people wait longer, but you are entitled to ask the hospital or your Integrated Care Board what options exist, including a different provider.

Ask about choosing a different provider

For routine first outpatient appointments you often have a choice of provider, which can mean a shorter wait. See our Right to Choose letter template and our guide to getting seen faster.

Track how you are doing

Keep a simple log of symptoms, flare-ups, and how they affect your work and sleep. It helps you spot a change, and it gives clinicians something concrete if you need an earlier slot. The Kaur Care brief includes a 5-day symptom log that goes straight into your one-page summary.

Speak up if things get worse

If your symptoms change, get worse, or new ones appear, tell your GP and ask whether your referral should be updated or made more urgent, and ask them to note it in your record. For anything severe or sudden, use NHS 111 or 999, you should not wait out an emergency.

Look after yourself in the gap

  • Ask your pharmacist about symptom relief and self-care, see what Pharmacy First covers.
  • Look into free support such as NHS Talking Therapies, which you can often self-refer to.
  • Charities for your condition often run helplines, forums and advice.
  • If you are considering paying for private care, our private care guide explains costs and how to return to the NHS afterwards.

Ask to be told if a slot opens up

Ask the booking team whether they keep a cancellation list and whether you can be added, and make sure they have an up-to-date phone number and your preferred times.

This is general information, not medical advice. Rules differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, see care across the UK.
