Skip the 8 AM GP Queue: How to Write an eConsult or NHS App Brief That Gets Actioned

6 min read · Kaur Care Journal

The 8 AM phone scramble is not the only way in any more. Most GP practices in England now accept online requests, through eConsult, Patchs, Anima or the NHS App, and a well-written one often gets triaged and actioned the same day. The trick is knowing what to put in it.

Why the written route can work better

When a clinician reads a clear, complete request, they can make a decision without a round of phone tag. A vague "please call me" gets queued; a focused brief with the right detail gets a plan. You are doing the triage team a favour, and helping yourself.

What to include

One clear ask

Say what you want up front: a prescription, a referral, advice, a sick note, test results. One request per submission works best.

The timeline

When it started, whether it is getting better or worse, and how fast.

Symptoms, specifically

Where, how bad (a 0 to 10 score helps), and anything that makes it better or worse. Vague beats nothing, but specific beats vague.

What you have already tried

Pharmacy remedies, pain relief, rest, previous treatments and whether they helped.

The impact

What you cannot do right now, work, sleep, school, caring. This helps the team judge urgency.

Your preference

Phone call, face to face, or happy with a message back.

A simple structure to copy: What I need → What is happening (and since when) → What I have tried → How it is affecting me → My questions. That is exactly the shape the Kaur Care GP brief produces.

Keep it to one issue

If you have three separate problems, resist the urge to list them all in one box. Triage systems handle one clear issue far better than a mixed bag; submit separately or flag that others can wait.

When not to use online triage

Online forms are for non-urgent problems. If symptoms are severe or fast-moving, chest pain, trouble breathing, a stiff neck with a high fever, heavy bleeding, confusion, use NHS 111 or call 999. When in doubt, 111 will tell you where to go.
