Surgical Instruments Procurement: A Supplier's Guide

Surgical instruments procurement spans everything from basic reusable instruments to high-value orthopaedic implants, energy devices and complete operating-theatre systems. It is a technically demanding, compliance-heavy category with long framework cycles.

How surgical instruments are bought

  • National frameworks (e.g. NHS Supply Chain) for reusable instruments and consumables
  • Capital tenders for theatre systems, imaging and energy platforms
  • Managed service and consignment arrangements for implants

Compliance and evaluation

Instruments used in surgery face strict regulatory and quality requirements. Buyers typically require:

  • CE / UKCA marking and conformity with the EU MDR / UK MDR
  • ISO 13485 quality management and full traceability
  • Sterilisation, reprocessing and decontamination evidence for reusables
  • Clinical evaluation and, for implants, outcomes/registry data

Finding surgical instrument tenders

Surgical tenders (CPV 33169000 surgical instruments, 33162000 theatre apparatus) appear across TED Europa, UK Find a Tender and NHS Supply Chain, plus US federal channels. Selling across regions means monitoring several portals, see selling medical devices to the NHS for the UK-specific path.

Frequently asked questions

What CPV codes cover surgical instruments?

Mainly 33169000 (surgical instruments) and 33162000 (operating theatre devices and apparatus).

What compliance do surgical instruments need?

CE/UKCA marking under the MDR, ISO 13485 quality management, full traceability, and decontamination evidence for reusable instruments.
