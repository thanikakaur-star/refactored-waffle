WHO Procurement vs UNICEF Supply Division: What's the Difference?

WHO Procurement and UNICEF Supply Division are both major global health buyers, but they play different roles: WHO procures primarily to support its own health programmes and technical guidance, while UNICEF Supply Division is the world's largest single buyer and distributor of vaccines and children's health supplies, procuring at massive scale for delivery to over 100 countries. For suppliers, understanding the difference shapes where to focus.

What is WHO Procurement?

The World Health Organization procures health products — medicines, diagnostics, vaccines, medical devices — both for its own operational use and to support member states, especially during health emergencies. WHO is also the body behind prequalification, the quality assessment that many other global buyers rely on before purchasing a product.

What is UNICEF Supply Division?

Based in Copenhagen, UNICEF Supply Division is one of the largest procurers of vaccines and essential health supplies in the world. It buys at enormous volume — vaccines, nutrition products, medical equipment, water and sanitation supplies — for distribution to children and families in over 100 countries. Its scale gives it significant negotiating power and a highly structured, competitive supplier approval process.

How they connect

The two are closely linked. WHO prequalification is often a prerequisite for UNICEF and other major buyers — a WHO-prequalified vaccine or medicine has effectively passed the quality bar that lets it be purchased at scale by UNICEF and similar agencies. So for many suppliers, the path runs: get WHO prequalified, then become eligible for UNICEF (and Gavi, and Global Fund) tenders.

Other major global health buyers to know

  • Gavi, The Vaccine Alliance — funds and coordinates vaccine access in lower-income countries
  • The Global Fund — finances programmes against HIV, TB and malaria, procuring related health products
  • Regional development banks (e.g. Asian Development Bank) — fund health infrastructure and supply projects

These bodies often coordinate closely, so a supplier active in one is frequently relevant to the others.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need WHO prequalification to sell to UNICEF?

For many product categories — particularly vaccines and essential medicines — yes, WHO prequalification is effectively required by UNICEF and similar large buyers.

Is UNICEF Supply Division only for vaccines?

No, though vaccines are a major category. It also procures nutrition products, medical equipment, and water/sanitation supplies.

Are WHO and UNICEF tenders published in the same place?

No — each operates its own procurement channels, which is part of why aggregating global health opportunities is genuinely difficult without a dedicated tool.
