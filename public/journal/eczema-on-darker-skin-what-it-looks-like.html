What Eczema Looks Like on Brown and Black Skin (and Why It Gets Missed)

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Pictures of eczema usually show red, inflamed skin. On brown and black skin it often looks different, which can make it easier to miss or to judge as milder than it feels. Knowing what to look for helps you describe it and ask for the right care.

How eczema can look on darker skin

The National Eczema Society notes that on pigmented skin eczema may look pink, red and purple, or like a subtle darkening of your usual skin colour. The NHS also notes that eczema can appear lighter or darker than the surrounding skin. Alongside colour, look for dryness, rough or thickened patches, scaling, and above all itching.

Where it appears

In many people the classic pattern is the creases of the elbows and knees. The National Eczema Society notes that in people with darker skin the pattern can be reversed, affecting the fronts of the knees and the outsides of the elbows. Eczema can also affect the face, neck, hands and eyelids.

Colour changes afterwards

After a flare, skin can stay lighter or darker than before, which is called post-inflammatory pigmentation change. It is more noticeable on darker skin, can take months to fade, and tends to improve once the eczema is well controlled. It is not caused by moisturiser, and it is worth mentioning to your clinician.

Why it can be missed or underestimated

Redness is one of the main ways clinicians judge how inflamed skin is, and it is harder to see on darker skin. Some research suggests the severity of eczema in skin of colour can be underestimated for this reason, and clinical trial guidance encourages assessing inflammation with the underlying skin tone in mind. This does not mean your clinician is not trying. It means it is worth giving them more than colour to go on.

How to describe it so you are heard

  • Itch: how bad it is and whether it wakes you at night.
  • Feel: dry, rough, thickened, warm or weeping skin.
  • Impact: sleep, work, school, mood and daily life.
  • Pattern: where it appears and how often it flares.
  • What you have tried: creams, ointments and anything that triggers it.

What helps

Daily moisturising is the foundation for everyone with eczema. Read more in our guide to affordable fragrance-free emollients. If your skin looks infected (weeping, crusting, hot and painful) or you feel unwell, seek medical advice promptly.

This article is general information, not a diagnosis. If you are unsure what a skin change is, see your GP or pharmacist.
