How to Respond to a Healthcare RFP: A Practical Guide

To respond well to a healthcare RFP (Request for Proposal), read the requirements in full before starting, answer every question in the format asked, evidence your claims rather than making generic statements, price using real market data, and submit early rather than at the deadline. RFPs are won or lost as much on compliance and clarity as on the underlying product. Here's a practical, step-by-step approach.

Step 1: Read everything before you write anything

Before drafting a single word, read the full RFP documentation, requirements, evaluation criteria, submission format, and deadlines. Rushing into writing before understanding exactly what's being scored is the most common way good suppliers lose to weaker ones who simply followed instructions better.

Step 2: Make a bid/no-bid decision

Not every RFP is worth responding to. Be honest about fit: can you meet the technical requirements, deliver on the timeline, and price competitively? A disciplined bid/no-bid decision protects your time for the RFPs you can actually win.

Step 3: Understand the evaluation criteria

Healthcare RFPs typically score responses against published criteria, often a mix of price, technical quality, experience, and compliance (increasingly including social value and sustainability). Structure your response to explicitly address each criterion, in the order it's asked, so evaluators can score you easily. Buried or implied answers score worse than direct ones.

Step 4: Answer with evidence, not adjectives

"We have extensive experience" is a claim. "We've delivered this product to 12 NHS trusts over 5 years with a 98% on-time delivery rate" is evidence. Evaluators score evidence. Wherever possible, back claims with data, case studies, and specifics relevant to the buyer's stated needs.

Step 5: Price with market data, not guesswork

Price is usually a major scoring factor. Rather than pricing on cost-plus alone, benchmark against what similar contracts have actually been awarded for, see our full guide on how to price a healthcare tender bid. Public award data shows the range winning bids land in, so you can price competitively without guessing.

Step 6: Don't miss the mandatory items

Missing a single mandatory document, certification, or format requirement can mean automatic disqualification, regardless of how strong the rest of your response is. Build a checklist from the RFP requirements and verify every item before submission.

Step 7: Submit early

Portal issues, file size limits, and last-minute reviews all eat time. Submitting a day early avoids technical problems costing you a bid you'd otherwise have won.

Common reasons RFP responses fail

  • Missing a mandatory requirement or certification
  • Generic answers that don't address the specific evaluation criteria
  • Pricing without reference to what the market actually pays
  • Submitting late or in the wrong format
  • Finding the RFP too late to prepare a strong response

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between an RFP and a tender?

The terms are often used interchangeably in public procurement. An RFP typically asks for a detailed proposal against stated requirements, while "tender" is the broader term for a competitive procurement process.

How long should an RFP response take to prepare?

It varies by complexity, but starting as early as possible after publication gives you the best chance to produce a strong, evidenced response rather than a rushed one.

Can I ask the buyer questions during an RFP process?

Most public RFPs include a clarification period where you can submit questions. Use it, it's a legitimate way to remove ambiguity before you commit to a response.
