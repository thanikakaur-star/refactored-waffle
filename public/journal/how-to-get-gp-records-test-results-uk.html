How to Get Your GP Records and Test Results (NHS App and Subject Access Request)

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Your health record belongs to the story of your care, and you are entitled to see it. Having your own results means fewer wasted appointments, better questions, and a clearer paper trail if you ever need to challenge a decision. Here are the main ways to get them in England.

1. The NHS App (the quickest)

If you register for the NHS App, you can usually see test results, medicines, appointments, and, where your practice has switched on online record access, entries in your GP record. Some practices release results as soon as they are filed, and others after a clinician reviews them. If something you expect is missing, ask your practice what is switched on.

2. Ask your practice for specific information

You can ask reception, or send an online message, for a particular result, a letter, or a summary of your record. Being specific makes it faster, for example "the blood test results from March" or "the hospital letter about my scan".

3. A subject access request (SAR)

You have a legal right to a copy of the personal data an organisation holds about you, including your health record. It is free, and the organisation must usually respond within one month, which can be extended for complex requests. You can ask your practice for your full record, or hospital records through the hospital's records or information governance team. Say what you need, whether a time period or a particular condition, to keep it focused.

What to do once you have it

  • Read it with a pen. Note anything unclear, and any facts that look wrong.
  • Bring a short list of questions to your next appointment.
  • If something factual is wrong, such as a date, medicine or allergy, ask for it to be corrected, and ask that your view be noted if you and the clinician disagree.

Use it to prepare

Your results can make the next appointment shorter and sharper. Put the key points on a single page, what the tests showed, your timeline, what you have tried, and what you want to ask, with the Kaur Care brief, and see our guide to preparing for a 10-minute GP appointment. If you are making a complaint or asking for a second opinion, your records are the evidence.

Privacy matters

Your record is sensitive. Only use secure routes, such as the NHS App or your practice's official request form, and be careful sharing screenshots. Kaur Care does not see or store anything you type into its tools, and you can read how that works in our privacy page.

This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rules and processes can differ across the UK.
