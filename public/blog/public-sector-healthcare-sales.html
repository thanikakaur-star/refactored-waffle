Public Sector Healthcare Sales: A Beginner's Guide

Selling to the public sector is a huge opportunity, but it runs on rules that catch out newcomers. The good news: those rules make it a level playing field, so a well-prepared small supplier can beat a bigger, complacent one.

Why public sector sales is different

Because it spends public money, the public sector must buy transparently and competitively. That means published tenders, set evaluation criteria and scored bids, rather than relationship-led selling. Your job is to be visible when the tender opens and compelling when it is scored.

The essentials to get right

  • Registrations and accreditations buyers expect in your sector
  • Frameworks and DPS that give you access, see framework vs DPS
  • A bid library of reusable answers, see building a bid team
  • Awareness of thresholds, see procurement thresholds

Finding your first opportunities

Public healthcare tenders are published on government portals, Find a Tender and Contracts Finder in the UK, TED across the EU, SAM.gov in the US, and the World Bank and UN globally. Monitoring them in one feed is the fastest way in, see winning government healthcare contracts and our tenders hub.

Winning your first contract

  • Bid selectively, quality over quantity
  • Answer the question that was asked, and address every criterion
  • Evidence outcomes and social value, not just price
  • Learn from every result, see common mistakes

Frequently asked questions

How do I start selling to the public sector?

Get the registrations buyers expect, join relevant frameworks or DPS, monitor published tenders, and respond selectively with strong, criteria-led bids.

Is public sector selling worth it for a small supplier?

Yes. The rules create a level playing field, so a well-prepared small supplier can win against larger competitors, especially on quality and social value.

Where do I find public sector healthcare tenders?

On government portals such as Find a Tender, Contracts Finder, TED and SAM.gov, plus World Bank and UN channels. Aggregators combine these into one feed.
