NHS Right to Choose Letter Template: Free Example to Send to Your GP

5 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

If you are in England and waiting for a routine specialist appointment, you may be able to choose which NHS-funded provider sees you, sometimes with a much shorter wait. Putting your request in writing makes it easier for your GP to action and leaves a record. Here is a free template you can copy, and how to use it.

What Right to Choose means

Under the NHS Constitution and the NHS Choice Framework, patients in England who are being referred for a first outpatient appointment have the right to choose the organisation that provides their care, where it is clinically appropriate to refer. The provider must hold an NHS contract for the service, so it stays free. It does not apply in emergencies, and the details differ for some services, so always confirm with your GP and the provider. It is an England-only right: Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own systems, see care across the UK.

Before you write

  1. Find a provider that explicitly accepts NHS Right to Choose referrals for the service you need and for your area, and confirm with them.
  2. Note their full name and location or website.
  3. Have your NHS number and GP surgery details to hand.

The template

Copy this, replace the square brackets, and send it through your surgery's online form, the NHS App, or by post.

Subject: Request for referral under NHS Right to Choose: [service]

Dear [GP's name],

I am writing to ask you to refer me for [service], and to exercise my right to choose the provider of my first outpatient appointment. Under the NHS Constitution and the NHS Choice Framework, patients in England have the right to choose the organisation that provides their care when they are referred for a first outpatient appointment, where it is clinically appropriate to refer.

A brief summary: [what is happening and for how long]. So far I have tried [treatments or self-care]. It is affecting [work, sleep, daily life].

The provider I would like to be referred to is [provider name, location]. I understand they accept NHS Right to Choose referrals for patients in my area, and I will confirm this with them.

Could you please make the referral to my chosen provider, let me know within 14 days that it has been sent, and tell me if you need any further information from me? If you feel a referral is not clinically appropriate, I would be grateful if you could tell me the reason in writing and record my request in my notes, so we can discuss the next steps.

Yours sincerely,
[Name], [date of birth], [NHS number]

After you send it

  • Keep a copy and note the date.
  • If you have not heard back by your deadline, follow up politely through the same route.
  • Once the referral is made, ask the provider to confirm they have received it.
  • If your GP declines, ask for the reason in writing, and see our guide on what to do if the answer is no.
