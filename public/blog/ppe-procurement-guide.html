PPE Procurement in Healthcare: A Supplier's Guide

Healthcare PPE procurement covers gloves, gowns, masks, respirators and other protective equipment bought by hospitals and public health systems, usually through national frameworks (NHS Supply Chain, EU public procurement) and, for lower-income markets, through WHO and UNICEF stockpiling programmes. Since 2020 it has become a far more structured, scrutinised category than it once was. Here's how it works and how suppliers should approach it.

Who buys PPE, and how

  • NHS Supply Chain — runs national PPE frameworks covering gloves, gowns, masks and more for all NHS trusts
  • TED Europa — EU member states run their own PPE frameworks and tenders, often at national or regional level
  • WHO & UNICEF — procure PPE at scale for emergency stockpiles and lower-income country distribution — see WHO vs UNICEF Supply Division
  • SAM.gov — US federal agencies (VA, DoD, HHS) procure PPE through their own contracts and stockpile programmes

Compliance requirements

PPE sold into healthcare settings has to meet defined safety standards, and buyers scrutinise this closely after well-publicised quality issues during the pandemic. Depending on the product and market, expect to demonstrate:

  • CE / UKCA marking appropriate to the PPE category
  • Relevant EN standards (e.g. EN 14683 for medical face masks, EN 455 for medical gloves)
  • Quality management certification and consistent batch testing
  • Robust supply chain traceability — buyers want to know exactly where PPE is manufactured

What's different about PPE procurement now

Pandemic-era emergency procurement (fast, high-volume, less scrutinised) has given way to a more normal, framework-driven market. That means:

  • Price is scrutinised again — benchmarking against real award data matters, see pricing a tender bid
  • Supply chain resilience is now formally evaluated — single-source, single-country supply chains are viewed as a risk
  • Sustainability increasingly matters — reusable and lower-environmental-impact PPE options are gaining evaluation weight

Finding PPE tenders

PPE tenders and frameworks are published across NHS, TED Europa, WHO and SAM.gov — each with its own portal, format and classification codes. A supplier selling across regions ends up monitoring several systems just for one product category, which is exactly the fragmentation problem covered in manual tracking vs a platform.

Frequently asked questions

What standards does medical PPE need to meet?

It depends on the product, but typically CE/UKCA marking plus relevant EN standards (e.g. EN 14683 for masks, EN 455 for gloves).

Is PPE procurement still high-volume post-pandemic?

Yes — PPE remains a core, continuously procured category, though the process has returned to normal competitive tendering rather than emergency purchasing.

Do WHO and UNICEF buy PPE directly from manufacturers?

Often yes, particularly for large stockpiling programmes, though they may also work through distributors depending on the market.
