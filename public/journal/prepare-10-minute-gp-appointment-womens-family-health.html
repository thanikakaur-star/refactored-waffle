How to Prepare for a 10-Minute GP Appointment (For Women's Health, Parents & Adults)

6 min read · Kaur Care Journal

Ten minutes is not long, especially when you are nervous or in pain and the thing you most wanted to say slips your mind. The good news is that a few minutes of preparation genuinely changes the outcome. Here is how to walk in ready, whatever the concern.

Before you go: get it on one page

Write down your main concern in a sentence, when it started, and how it has changed. Pick the one or two things that matter most, and lead with those. Bring any relevant dates, measurements or a symptom diary. If you take medicines, have the list to hand.

If writing under pressure is not your thing, the Kaur Care 1-page GP brief turns a few answers into a tidy summary you can hand over or read from.

For women's health

These appointments are often rushed, so specifics help you be heard. Note your cycle length and how regular it is; if bleeding is the issue, how heavy (flooding, clots, products used per hour) and how it affects your energy. For pain, where it is, a 0 to 10 score at its worst, and how it tracks with your cycle. Say what you have already tried, and what you want to discuss, including options like hormonal treatment or a referral. Tiredness with heavy periods is worth flagging, as it can prompt an iron check.

For parents

Bring the timeline: when symptoms started, temperature readings, feeding or drinking, wet nappies, rashes, and anything unusual about behaviour or energy. Note what has helped. Trust your instinct, if your child seems very unwell, say so plainly, and remember that for serious or fast-changing symptoms 111 and 999 are there day and night.

For any adult

Give the shape of it: when it began, what triggers or eases it, and the impact on work and sleep. Write your questions down in advance, because that is what tends to get forgotten.

During the appointment

Lead with your main concern in the first sentence. Ask your questions while you are there. Before you leave, confirm the plan: what happens next, what to watch for, and when to come back or seek urgent help.

After

Jot down what was agreed, any medicine names and the follow-up date, while it is fresh. It makes the next step, and the next appointment, far easier.
