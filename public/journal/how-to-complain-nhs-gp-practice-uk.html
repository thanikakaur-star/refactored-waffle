How to Complain About Your GP Practice or the NHS (Step by Step, UK)

6 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Complaining is not about causing trouble. It is how problems get noticed, fixed, and stopped from happening to someone else. If something has gone wrong, such as a missed diagnosis, being dismissed, a long unexplained delay, or poor communication, you have a clear route. This guide covers England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own processes, so check yours.

Step 1: Decide what you want

Do you want an explanation, an apology, a change to your care, or a change to how the service works? Knowing this helps you write clearly and helps the practice respond properly.

Step 2: Raise it early and informally, if you feel able

A conversation with the practice manager, or the service's Patient Advice and Liaison Service (PALS) for hospitals, often solves things quickly. It is fine to skip this step if it feels unsafe or you have already tried it.

Step 3: Make a formal complaint

  • Who to send it to: the organisation involved (the practice, hospital or provider), or the organisation that commissions the service, which for GP care is generally your Integrated Care Board (ICB). Some primary care services are handled through NHS England's customer contact centre.
  • Time limit: usually within 12 months of the incident, or of when you first became aware of the problem. Time limits can sometimes be extended with good reason.
  • Format: you can complain in writing or verbally, and in most cases the organisation must acknowledge it within three working days. They should investigate and send you a written response.

What to write

  1. Facts and dates. What happened, when, and who was involved, kept plain and in order.
  2. Impact. What it meant for your health, work or family.
  3. What you want. An explanation, an apology, a change, a referral.
  4. A reply deadline, and your contact details.

Keep a copy and a note of every call and email. Our referral and request letter builder uses the same clear, calm structure, and the SBAR approach helps you set out events in order.

Free support

Independent NHS complaints advocacy is available free in England, and your local Healthwatch can point you to it. Citizens Advice can also help you plan what to write.

If you are not satisfied with the response

You can take it to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman (PHSO), which is free and independent, once you have had a final response from the organisation or if the complaint is taking too long. In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland the equivalent ombudsman is a different body.

If you are worried about your care now

A complaint is about the past. If you are unwell right now, seek care first: NHS 111 for urgent advice, 999 in an emergency. You can also ask for your records and results to support your complaint.

This is general information, not legal advice. Details can change, so check current NHS guidance.
