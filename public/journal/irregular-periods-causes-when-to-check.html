Irregular Periods: Common Causes and When to Get Checked

4 min read · The Kaur Care Journal

Periods that arrive early, late, or skip a month can be unsettling. Often there is a straightforward explanation, but some changes are worth checking so nothing is missed.

Common reasons cycles shift

Stress, significant weight change, intense exercise, travel, some contraception, thyroid issues and approaching perimenopause can all affect regularity. In your 40s, more variable cycles are often an early sign of perimenopause.

When to see your GP

Book an appointment if your periods suddenly become very irregular when that is not normal for you, stop for several months (and you are not pregnant or using contraception that does this), or come with other symptoms like significant weight or mood changes. Always get bleeding after the menopause checked.

What helps the conversation

Track your cycle dates over a few months, note any other changes, and mention any family history. This helps your GP decide whether checks, such as blood tests, would be useful.

When to seek help sooner

Very heavy bleeding, feeling faint, or severe pelvic pain need prompt attention, contact your GP or NHS 111, and 999 for severe symptoms.
