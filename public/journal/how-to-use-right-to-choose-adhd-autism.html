How to Use NHS Right to Choose for ADHD & Autism Assessments in England

7 min read · Kaur Care Journal

If you have been told the wait for an ADHD or autism assessment is measured in years, you are not imagining it. What far fewer people are told is that in England you have a legal right to choose who assesses you, and that choice can dramatically shorten the wait, at no cost to you.

What Right to Choose actually is

Under the NHS Choice Framework in England, when your GP refers you for a routine assessment you can usually ask to be referred to any provider that holds an NHS contract for that service, not only your local one. Several independent providers offer NHS-funded ADHD and autism assessments with shorter waiting lists. You still pay nothing; it is NHS care, just delivered somewhere with more capacity.

Right to Choose applies to routine referrals in England. It does not apply in urgent or specialist mental-health crisis situations, and local rules can vary, so always confirm current acceptance with your GP and the provider.

Step by step

1. Book a GP appointment about the referral

You need a GP referral to start. Be clear that you would like to be assessed for ADHD or autism and that you want to use your right to choose your provider.

2. Choose a provider that holds an NHS contract

Do a little homework first. Look for a provider that explicitly offers NHS Right to Choose assessments for your nation and age group, and note their name and address so the GP can refer directly.

3. Ask for the referral to that provider

Give the GP the provider's details. The referral itself is a standard NHS e-referral; it does not cost you anything.

4. If the GP is unsure or declines

Some practices are simply unfamiliar with the process, and local Integrated Care Board (ICB) policies do vary. It often helps to calmly mention that you are exercising your right under the NHS Choice Framework, and to bring a short written request. If it still stalls, ask what the practice's policy is and whether the practice manager can help.

5. Put it in writing

A short, clear letter that names the framework, the condition and your chosen provider makes the request easy to action and easy to file. You can build one in a couple of minutes with the Kaur Care referral-letter tool, then hand or email it to your surgery.

What to expect after referral

Waiting times still vary by provider, so it is worth comparing before you choose. Once referred, the provider will contact you about next steps and any questionnaires. Keep a note of dates and who you spoke to.

A note on expectations

Right to Choose is a route, not a guarantee of a particular outcome, and acceptance can depend on your ICB. But for a lot of people it is the single most effective way to turn a multi-year wait into a far shorter one.
